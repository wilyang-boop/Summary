Vehicle Integration Package

One baseline binds the whole stack.

A VIP is the validated release configuration for a vehicle program — the compatibility contract that ties software, firmware, and coding into a single thing you can flash, test, sign off, and ship to fleet. Not a file. A proven combination.

04 / Why VIPs churn

A new VIP is rarely a rename

A VIP is refreshed whenever any one of its coupled ingredients moves — each is an independent compatibility axis. Expand any axis to see what a change usually means in practice.

Why an NDAS upgrade drags in a VIP

NDAS lives inside a broader validated baseline. Coding fails when the VIP and DDU versions don't match, and NDAS-only bundles may fail in the pipeline — the guidance is to use the full target SW bundle. Moving NDAS alone is sometimes technically possible but operationally unsupported, so teams move it as a VIP.

Why Aurix churns constantly

Aurix is both a compatibility dependency and a source of release risk. A regressed release can break clear-persistency or flashing; aurixreset is routine during coding; fleet history is full of "New Aurix version" and "Point fix for Aurix stability." It can force a new VIP even when the feature story hasn't changed.
